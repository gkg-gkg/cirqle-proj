How we verify a claim

Every check a claim passes before a penny leaves your wallet — what each one catches, and what it does not. This is the long version of the tiles on the brands page.

The six checks

They run in order, and a claim that fails one does not continue to the next. Nothing here depends on you spotting anything.

The receipt is matched to yours

Every brand gives us one clean reference receipt from its own till at setup. Every receipt a member uploads against your deals is read and compared to it — store name, address, phone number, VAT number, and the visual format of the receipt itself. Receipts from a given chain share a shape: the same header block, the same column positions, the same footer. A receipt from somewhere else does not have that shape, and a receipt from nowhere at all rarely gets it right.

What it catches
Receipts from a different shop, and generated receipts that do not reproduce your format.
What it does not
A receipt from one of your own tills that was never actually paid for. That is what the next two checks are for.
The arithmetic is recomputed

Line items are added against the subtotal, and subtotal plus tax against the total. This happens in code, not by a model — the numbers are extracted, then arithmetic is done on them. It is deliberately the dullest check in the list and one of the most effective, because fake receipt generators reproduce how a receipt looks and routinely get its sums wrong. The same pass checks the purchase fits the deal: a plausible item, a sane date, and at least your minimum spend.

What it catches
Fabricated receipts whose totals do not reconcile, plus wrong-date and under-minimum claims.
What it does not
A real receipt for a real purchase whose numbers are correct — because they are.
No receipt is paid twice

A byte-identical re-upload is rejected outright before it is ever scored. The harder case is the same receipt saved again, cropped, or re-photographed off a screen — a different file, the same purchase. For those we compare a perceptual fingerprint of the image, so near-identical pictures are caught even when no two bytes match. Both checks run across every account, not just within one, so the same receipt cannot fund claims from several profiles.

What it catches
The same purchase claimed twice, by one person or by several people working together.
What it does not
Two genuinely different purchases that happen to look alike. Those are flagged rather than refused, and a person decides.
The post has to name you

We read the accounts tagged in the post and check them against the deal being claimed. A post that tags no brand cannot be matched to your deal. A post tagging a different Cirqle brand is rejected outright — you never pay for content that promoted a competitor. The post also has to stay up until the claim is checked; content taken down before we see it is content you did not get.

What it catches
Claims against the wrong brand, claims with no post behind them, and posts deleted before review.
What it does not
A post whose tags we were never able to read. Unknown stays unknown, and never counts against the member.
Bought audiences are scored down

Accounts are scored on follower-to-following ratio, engagement rate, and consistency across recent posts. Each signal is weak alone and telling together. Following far more accounts than follow back is the follow-farm signature. Engagement far below what an account that size should draw suggests the followers were bought; far above it, or near-identical post after post, suggests an engagement pod. A real account with three hundred real followers scores better than a bought one with thirty thousand.

What it catches
Bought followers, follow-farms and engagement pods.
What it does not
A small genuine account. Size is not what is being scored — the relationship between the numbers is.
Referral bonuses cannot be farmed

A referral bonus needs both people identity-verified through Stripe before either is paid, and is blocked when two accounts share bank or identity details. Those are compared as hashed fingerprints, so the underlying details are never held in a form we could read. On top of that, the referrer’s post has to predate the friend’s purchase — you cannot claim to have influenced a decision already made — and it only counts on that friend’s first claim for the deal.

What it catches
One person referring themselves from a second account, and bonus-stacking on repeat claims.
What it does not
Two people in one household with genuinely separate identities and bank accounts. The checks fail closed, so an unclear case is held rather than paid.

The human backstop

Automated checks are good at the cases they were built for and blind to the ones nobody has thought of yet. So a set of patterns is surfaced for review regardless of whether the checks above passed:

  • The same deal claimed over and over by one member.
  • A claim filed minutes after the account was created.
  • One receipt image appearing across more than one account.
  • One post backing claims from several different accounts.

Every one of those has an innocent explanation, which is exactly why they are flags and not rejections. They are reviewed by a person before any money moves — held, rather than paid first and questioned later.

What this is not

It is not image forensics. We do not claim to detect that a real receipt has been edited — a skilfully altered receipt whose numbers still reconcile will read as fine to the automated checks. Anyone telling you they catch that reliably from a phone photo is overselling.

That limitation is the reason the list above is a list. The duplicate fingerprinting, the brand-tag matching, the account scoring and the human review all exist because no single check can carry the weight. An edited receipt still has to survive being a plausible purchase, being unique across every account, having a real post behind it that tags you, and coming from an account that does not look bought. That is a far harder thing to do than editing a number.

What a failed claim costs you

Nothing. A claim that fails verification never reaches your wallet — it is not deducted and then refunded, not held against your balance, and not reconciled later. Your funded balance is your ceiling, and only approved claims draw against it.

You can see every claim and its state in your portal, including the ones that did not pass. You do not have to act on any of them.
