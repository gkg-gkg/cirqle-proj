Every claim is checked

You are paying for purchases, so the purchase has to be real. Every claim goes through the same checks, in the same order, and a claim that fails one never reaches your budget. You are not charged for it, and you do not have to spot it yourself. Tap any of them for the detail.

Every uploaded receipt is read and compared against the reference receipt you gave us at setup — store name, address, phone number, VAT number, and the visual format of the receipt itself. A receipt that did not come from one of your tills does not look like one of your receipts.

Compared against your own reference receipt

Line items are added against the subtotal, and subtotal plus tax against the total — in code, not by a model. This is what catches fabricated receipts: generators reproduce how a receipt looks and then routinely get its sums wrong. We also check the purchase fits the deal — a plausible item, a sane date, and at least your minimum spend.

Fails the maths, fails the claim

A byte-identical upload is rejected at the door. A re-saved, cropped or re-photographed copy is caught too, because we fingerprint what the image looks like, not just the file. The same check runs across accounts, so one receipt cannot quietly fund claims from several profiles.

Exact and near-duplicate detection

We read the accounts tagged in the post and check them against the deal being claimed. A post tagging a different Cirqle brand is rejected outright — you never pay for content that promoted someone else. The post also has to stay up until the claim is checked, so the content you paid for is content that actually ran.

Brand tag matched to your deal

Accounts are scored on follower-to-following ratio, engagement rate, and consistency across recent posts. Engagement far below what an account that size should draw reads as bought followers; far above it, or near-identical post after post, reads as an engagement pod. You are reaching real people, not a follower count someone bought.

Account quality scored on every claim

A referral bonus needs both people identity-verified through Stripe, and is blocked when two accounts share bank or identity details — compared as hashed fingerprints, so the details themselves are never held. The referrer’s post has to predate the friend’s purchase, and it only counts on that friend’s first claim for the deal. One person with two accounts earns nothing.
