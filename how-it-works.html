Sharing, on social media or in-person

Every claim is checked

Cashback is real money, and it comes out of a pot the brand has funded. Every pound someone takes with a fake receipt or a bought-follower account is a pound that isn't there for the people who actually shopped and actually shared. So we check — every claim, the same way, every time. None of this is aimed at you. It is what keeps the rates worth having.

Your receipt is read, not glanced at

Every brand on Cirqle gives us a reference receipt when they join. Yours is compared against it — store name, address, phone number, VAT number and the layout of the receipt itself. A receipt that didn't come from that brand doesn't look like that brand's receipts, and it shows.

Checked against the brand's own reference receipt
The numbers have to add up

We add the line items up against the subtotal, and the subtotal plus tax against the total — in code, not by eye. That is the part that catches made-up receipts. Receipt generators copy how a receipt looks, and then routinely get its sums wrong. We also check the purchase fits the deal: the right sort of item, a date that makes sense, and at least the minimum spend.

Arithmetic recomputed on every receipt
One receipt, one claim

The same receipt can't be claimed twice, by you or by anyone else. An identical upload is rejected outright, and a re-saved, cropped or re-photographed copy is caught too — we fingerprint what the image looks like, not just the file. The same goes across accounts: one receipt turning up on two profiles gets both of them looked at.

Duplicate and near-duplicate images blocked
The post has to be real, and has to stay up

We read the accounts tagged in your post and check them against the deal you claimed. Tag a different Cirqle brand and the claim is turned down there and then. And the post needs to stay up until we've checked it — a post deleted before then is one we can't verify, so the claim can be withheld.

Brand tag matched to the deal you claimed
Bot and bought-follower accounts are scored

We look at the shape of the account, not just its size: follower-to-following ratio, engagement rate, and how consistent it is across recent posts. Engagement far below what an account that size should get reads as bought followers. Engagement far above it, or engagement that is suspiciously identical post after post, reads as a pod. A real account with 300 real followers scores better than a bought one with 30,000.

Nothing to do if your account is genuine
You can't refer yourself

Referral bonuses need both people identity-verified through Stripe, and are blocked when two accounts share bank or identity details — compared as fingerprints, so we never hold the details themselves. Your post also has to come before your friend's purchase, and it only counts on their first claim for that deal. A second account of your own gets you nothing.

Stripe identity verification on both sides

On top of all that, anything that still looks odd — the same deal claimed over and over, a claim filed minutes after the account was made, one image or one post backing several accounts — goes to a person before any money moves. Not through. There is no version of this where a fake claim quietly pays out.

What it means if you're genuine: keep the post up until your claim is approved, use each receipt once, and share before your friend buys. Do that and there is nothing here to think about — your cashback clears three days after the date you posted, as normal.
